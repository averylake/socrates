01
The Work Socrates left no written work of his own. These two studies rebuild and then collapse his face through the systems that preserve him, flatten him, and make him available to us.
Two 1/1s
Moving image
2026

Socrates left no written work of his own. He reaches us through the alphabet he mistrusted.

Study 1 rebuilds his face through the systems that displaced him: binary rain coheres into his portrait; Greek letters emerge from the noise; phonetic residue of his name lingers before dissolving.

Study 2 — the Squeeze inverts the gesture. His face is already present, fully formed, filling the frame. Then, slowly, particle by particle, it is pulled toward the eight Greek letters of his name: Σ Ω Κ Ρ Α Τ Η Σ. What remains is only the symbol.

Together they form a diptych: emergence and collapse. The face assembles from code; the face dissolves back into it. Socrates warned that the alphabet would flatten us. These studies make him prove it on himself.

Artist Statement A focused moving study from the research field of When the Alphabet Becomes Strange, developed in relation to orality, writing, LLMs, and the inner life.
Statement

The shift from orality to writing was not only a technological transition. It reorganized human experience.

Writing gave us record, distance, abstraction, and the capacity to think across time. It also made its own losses difficult to name, because the tradition that replaced orality became the water we swim in.

Socrates saw something real. In the Phaedrus, writing appears as a technology that can kill memory and presence, giving the appearance of wisdom without its living substance.

Large language models do not simply automate text production. They make the alphabetic tradition strange. A text is no longer only a fixed record. It becomes responsive, conversational, unstable.

The question is not whether AI will restore what writing diminished. The question is whether this disruption can make us conscious enough to choose what should not be delegated.

Socrates warned the alphabet could flatten us. Now the binary continues. Maybe that is the invitation: to remember what could never be captured.

04
Lineage This work is adjacent to Signatures Through Technologies, but it is not the same object. The fingerprint becomes a philosopher's face, and the chiasm becomes a question about language itself.
Parent field
Companion study

Signatures Through Technologies maps the human signature across a 22-state chiasm: breath, trace, ink, print, code, prompt, light, interface, simulation, luminescence, and silence.

This Socrates work narrows the field. Instead of a fingerprint passing through technologies, the oral philosopher is reconstructed through the systems that displaced him, then collapsed back into the symbol that names him.

The result is not an answer. It is a disturbance: a face made from the very media that keep it at a distance, squeezed into eight letters, glitching at the end.

See also: When the Alphabet Becomes Strange: Apple, a companion study in which a red apple passes through binary and the alphabet before crumbling into silence.
